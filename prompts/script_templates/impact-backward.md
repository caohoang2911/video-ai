# Pattern: Impact Backward

Open with the aftermath — the human cost, a memorial, a legal consequence, a lasting
cultural scar — then rewind to trace the causes back to the decisions that produced
it, closing the loop by returning to the present-day impact.

Structure:
1. **Hook** (0-30s) — open in the aftermath: a memorial, a court case, a lingering
   consequence still visible today. Make the scale of impact concrete before the
   audience even knows the ship's name.
2. **Rewind** — go back to the beginning: the ship, the people, the setting.
3. **Build to the event** — walk forward again through the causes and the disaster
   itself, now that the audience knows what it cost.
4. **Return to the present** — close by coming back to the opening image/consequence,
   now fully explained — this closing loop is itself a strong final payoff node.

This pattern works best when the disaster's legacy (a law changed, a community
altered, a mystery still debated) is as compelling as the disaster itself — lean into
that legacy for the angle you were given.
